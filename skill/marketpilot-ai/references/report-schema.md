# Fixed report schema

Use this order for every industry. Add industry-specific detail within a section; do not create a competing top-level section unless the client explicitly requests it.

## Cover

Localize every cover label into the confirmed report language.

- Localized equivalent of `MARKET ENTRY ASSESSMENT`
- Company/brand
- Origin to target geography
- Timing and budget
- Disclaimer meaning, rendered fluently in the confirmed report language: `Generated from publicly available information and analytical estimates. Before making major decisions or committing capital, corroborate key findings with paid data sources, primary research, and qualified legal, financial, and local market advisors.`
- Research cut-off

## Table of Contents

- Place a localized, one-page table of contents on page 2, immediately after the cover.
- Include `Executive Summary & Decision`, Sections 1–12, and `Sources & Evidence Base`, using the exact localized section titles from the report.
- Show accurate page numbers and keep the contents page concise enough that the next section begins on page 3.
- Do not include subsection-level entries unless the client explicitly asks for a detailed contents page.

## Executive Summary & Decision

- Decision, score, launch market, entry mode, budget
- Concise rationale
- Management decision required

## 1. Confirmed Inputs

Use one two-column table equivalent to `Input | Confirmed assumption`, with both headers localized into the confirmed report language.

Rows, in this order:

1. Brand/company
2. Product/category
3. Origin market
4. Target market and first city, if confirmed
5. Entry timing
6. Budget and currency
7. Entry mode constraints
8. Localization or other constraints
9. Origin-market consumer profile
10. Origin-market pricing context

The last two rows are Step 0 public-company context. Keep them evidence-based and concise. If reliable official information is unavailable, say so in the table. Never move either row into a separate section.

## 2. Quick Blocker Scan

Use a conclusion-based form in every case, not a table:

- Begin with one concise overall blocker conclusion.
- Follow with separate bullets covering foreign-investment or market access; licensing, product, data, customs, employment, or operating blockers as relevant; and the decision consequence or required action.

## 3. Target Audience

- Customer Segment Map: identify at least two distinct credible segments and normally three to five when the evidence supports them. Define segments by need, occasion, behavior, geography, channel, or economics—not demographics alone.
- Segment Prioritization: compare each segment's core need or occasion, market attractiveness, accessibility, brand fit, economics, competitive intensity, and intended role.
- Primary Segment Selection: choose one segment and explain why it outranks the alternatives. State the evidence, decision criteria, and principal trade-off; do not merely label a segment `Primary`.
- Primary Segment Needs
- Brand Value
- Reason to Believe
- Research Evidence
- Secondary Segment Roles

## 4. Market Size and Demand Frame

- Market definition
- TAM / SAM / SOM
- Demand-side model
- Geographic opportunity
- Category growth and maturity
- Evidence quality and gaps

Preserve the TAM / SAM / SOM analytical structure, but localize the section title, `Layer` header, and full layer names into the confirmed report language. Do not leave English-only labels in a non-English report. Use acronym-only cells only when the client explicitly requests them.

For an English report, label the `Layer` rows exactly as:

- `Total Addressable Market`
- `Serviceable Available Market`
- `Serviceable Obtainable Market`

For a Chinese report, use the full names `总可寻址市场`, `可服务市场`, and `可获得市场`; the acronyms may appear once in the section title or first explanation. Apply the equivalent full localized names for other report languages.

## 5. Competitive Landscape

For each material competitor or alternative: type, target customer, positioning, competitive advantage, and threat/implication.

- Direct and structural competitors
- Need-state and price benchmarks where relevant
- Status quo / “Do Nothing” alternative
- Competitive White Space

## 6. Positioning & Pricing Recommendation

- Recommended positioning
- Competitive price benchmarks
- Recommended test range or price architecture
- Landed-cost, channel-fee, promotion, willingness-to-pay, and margin caveats

## 7. Product and Localization Strategy

- Retain
- Test locally

## 8. SWOT & Red Flags

- Use separate localized labels for Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, and Threats.
- Put every factor on its own bullet or line; never join multiple factors with semicolons in one paragraph or cell.
- Follow the four groups with a separately labeled Red Flags list.

## 9. Feasibility Score

- State that the assessment is Discovery-stage.
- Market Demand Potential
- Product-Market Fit
- Competitive Landscape
- Regulatory & Entry Risk
- Entry Cost & Operational Complexity
- For each dimension, show weight, evidence-supported score, evidence status, and weighted score. Use the explicit anchors and evidence rules in [feasibility-scoring.md](feasibility-scoring.md).
- List material `Unknown / Unverified` items separately. Never replace a missing fact with a neutral score. If an entire dimension is not scorable, do not silently renormalize the other weights or publish a false-precision single composite.
- Show the base weighted composite before policy support.
- Show each favorable-policy bonus separately, including the policy, eligibility, timing, economic mechanism, source, and points awarded. Do not award points for proposed, expired, inaccessible, or eligibility-uncertain programs.
- Show the final policy-adjusted score and decision. Calculate at full precision, cap at `10.0`, then round only the final displayed score to the nearest 0.5 point; exact quarter-point ties round upward. Display one decimal place.

## 10. Proposed Budget Allocation

Use the confirmed budget and currency. Allocate by workstream and reconcile exactly to the total.

## 11. Recommendations

- Go-to-Market Strategy
- Partner Strategy, only if relevant

## 12. Action Plan

- Entry Mode Comparison
- Phased actions, gates, and decision points
- Year-one objective or equivalent outcome

## Sources & Evidence Base

Localize this section title, every source-group heading, every source annotation, and every visible link label into the confirmed report language.

- English-language sources, when relevant
- Target-market local-language sources, labeled with the actual language name
- Live-source verification, conflicting estimates, and evidence gaps

If the target market's primary research language is English, one English-language source list is sufficient; do not create a duplicate local-language list. In a Chinese report, for example, use headings such as `英文信源`, `中文信源`, and `实时信源核验、冲突估算与证据缺口`. Annotate each source with the fact or judgment it supports. Prefer official and primary sources, then strong secondary reporting. Do not hide contradictions.

Each source entry must contain:

- a title or description localized into the confirmed report language;
- the publication or issuing organization and date when available;
- a concise note stating what the source supports;
- a visible localized link label, such as `查看原文` in Chinese, linked to the exact original page rather than a search-results page or generic home page.

Before delivery, check that every intended hyperlink exists in the generated Word file as an external relationship and resolves to the intended source when access permits. A source without a usable link must be labeled transparently and cannot be the sole support for a decision-critical claim.

Also check claim-to-source alignment. The original page must explicitly support the attributed fact and stated geography. For shipping, delivery, store availability, licensing, or market-presence claims, require the source to name the relevant country or city; do not infer a specific market from generic statements such as `international shipping` or `global availability`.

## Whole-document language rule

The confirmed report language applies to all user-facing content, including titles, table headers and cells, chart labels, callouts, captions, notes, and source labels. Official proper nouns, registered names, model names, and widely used acronyms may remain, but explain unfamiliar terms in plain language on first use. Run a terminology and fluency pass to remove literal translations, unexplained local jargon, inconsistent terminology, and residual template language before rendering.
