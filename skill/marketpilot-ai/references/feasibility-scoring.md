# Discovery-stage feasibility scoring

Use this rubric for every MarketPilot case. It measures whether a market-entry opportunity merits validation at the Discovery stage. It does not assume that the company already has local pilots, signed partners, approvals, or launch infrastructure.

## Base score

Use these fixed weights:

| Dimension | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Market Demand Potential | 30% |
| Product-Market Fit | 25% |
| Competitive Landscape | 20% |
| Regulatory & Entry Risk | 15% |
| Entry Cost & Operational Complexity | 10% |

Higher scores always mean more favorable feasibility. For `Regulatory & Entry Risk` and `Entry Cost & Operational Complexity`, a high score means lower or more manageable risk and complexity.

Score against the anchors below. Use intermediate whole or half-point scores only when the evidence clearly falls between anchors. Do not gravitate to the midpoint merely because the case is uncertain.

### Market Demand Potential

| Score | Discovery-stage anchor |
|---:|---|
| 0–2 | Little relevant demand, structural decline, or no practically reachable segment. |
| 4 | Demand exists but is small, weak, declining, poorly defined, or supported mainly by a broad TAM. |
| 6 | Credible current demand and a plausible reachable segment are supported by verified secondary or official evidence, but geographic, channel, price-band, or bottom-up demand remains incomplete. |
| 8 | Strong current demand, attractive growth or maturity, a clearly reachable segment, credible geographic and channel concentration, and a defensible bottom-up SOM are supported by multiple current sources. |
| 10 | Exceptional, sustained and well-evidenced demand with highly favorable category momentum, strong target-segment economics, multiple access routes, and minimal demand-side ambiguity. |

### Product-Market Fit

| Score | Discovery-stage anchor |
|---:|---|
| 0–2 | The core proposition conflicts with local needs, price expectations, usage conditions, or required localization. |
| 4 | Some need fit exists, but price, product form, trust, channel, localization, or consumer belief creates a substantial mismatch. |
| 6 | The proposition plausibly addresses a local need and comparable products show demand, but willingness to pay, transferability, or required adaptation remains materially unverified. |
| 8 | Strong need-state fit, credible price architecture, limited or manageable adaptation, transferable evidence from comparable markets, and a persuasive consumer-facing Reason to Believe. |
| 10 | Compelling and unusually well-supported fit across need, usage, price, trust and channel, with direct target-market evidence or equivalent proof and very limited adaptation risk. |

### Competitive Landscape

| Score | Discovery-stage anchor |
|---:|---|
| 0–2 | Entrenched competitors dominate access and economics; the entrant has no meaningful differentiation or white space. |
| 4 | The market is crowded and the proposition is largely substitutable; competitors hold clear brand, price, channel or capability advantages. |
| 6 | A credible white space or differentiation exists, but it is narrow, copyable, difficult to communicate, or dependent on uncertain channel access. |
| 8 | Clear and relevant white space, favorable competitive benchmarks, differentiated value that matters to the primary segment, and realistic access despite incumbent responses. |
| 10 | A rare, durable and highly relevant advantage with weak incumbent answers, favorable status-quo comparison, attractive economics and multiple defensible access routes. |

### Regulatory & Entry Risk

| Score | Discovery-stage anchor |
|---:|---|
| 0–2 | A structural prohibition, unresolved legal barrier, incompatible product requirement, or highly adverse trade regime makes entry impractical. |
| 4 | A legal path may exist, but major approvals, ownership, customs, data, licensing or product obligations are unresolved and could invalidate the case. |
| 6 | Entry appears legally feasible through known processes, but several material requirements, costs, timelines or eligibility questions still require professional confirmation. |
| 8 | A clear and conventional entry path exists, no structural blocker is evident, material obligations are mapped, and remaining confirmation work is manageable. |
| 10 | The market is unusually open and predictable for this entrant and product, with low compliance burden, clear eligibility, limited trade friction and minimal timing risk. |

### Entry Cost & Operational Complexity

| Score | Discovery-stage anchor |
|---:|---|
| 0–2 | Minimum viable entry is economically impractical or depends on capabilities and infrastructure the company cannot reasonably obtain. |
| 4 | Entry is capital-heavy, operationally complex, or dependent on multiple unproven partners, supply chains, service systems or unit-economics assumptions. |
| 6 | Entry is feasible with the available or proposed resources, but material operating dependencies, landed economics, service capacity or partner requirements remain. |
| 8 | The company can fund the minimum viable entry, transfer relevant capabilities, obtain required partners, and support credible unit economics with manageable complexity. |
| 10 | Entry is comparatively light, highly executable and well within the company's proven capabilities, with strong supply, service, partner and economic readiness. |

## Unknown and evidence-status rules

Do not convert missing evidence into `5`, `6`, or any other default score.

For every dimension, assign one evidence status:

- `High`: decision-critical claims are verified through current primary or strong authoritative evidence and no material unknown is likely to change the anchor materially.
- `Medium`: the core basis is verified, but one or more material unknowns could move the dimension by approximately one point.
- `Low`: the score relies substantially on proxies, comparable markets, broad data, or unresolved assumptions that could move it by more than one point.
- `Not Scorable`: there is no adequate verified basis for a numerical dimension score.

List every material missing fact as `Unknown / Unverified` and state which dimension and decision it could affect.

- An unknown receives no numerical value.
- Do not average an unknown with known evidence.
- Do not lower a score merely because evidence is missing; reflect missing evidence through the status and unknown list.
- Do not raise a score based on an optimistic assumption.
- If an entire dimension is `Not Scorable`, do not renormalize the remaining weights. Publish `Insufficient Evidence for a Single Composite`, or a clearly labeled provisional range, and identify the research required to make it scorable.
- A numerical composite with any `Low`-evidence dimension must be labeled `Provisional`.
- A decision-critical unknown prevents an unconditional `Go`, even when the numerical score is high. Use `Investigate` or `Conditional Go` and make resolution of the unknown a gate.

## Favorable Policy Conditions bonus

Add a separately disclosed bonus after the base weighted composite. The total bonus may range from `0.0` to `+1.0`, and the final adjusted score is capped at `10.0`.

Eligible mechanisms include current government grants, customer subsidies, tax credits or exemptions, subsidized financing, public-procurement preferences, infrastructure co-funding, or other enacted support that directly improves demand, cost, access, or minimum viable entry economics.

| Bonus | Required condition |
|---:|---|
| +0.0 | No material verified support, or eligibility/timing is Unknown. |
| +0.25 | Limited, indirect, narrowly available, short-lived, or modest support with verified eligibility. |
| +0.50 | Material current support accessible to the entrant or primary segment that meaningfully improves demand or economics. |
| +0.75 | Strong and accessible support, or multiple complementary mechanisms, with a substantial and durable economic effect. |
| +1.00 | Exceptional, confirmed and sufficiently durable support that materially changes the market-entry economics or adoption curve. |

Award a bonus only when all of the following are verified on a current official source:

1. The policy is enacted and currently available, not merely proposed or announced as a target.
2. It applies to the exact target geography, industry, product and relevant entry period.
3. The entrant, its proposed local structure, or the primary target customer is eligible.
4. The support is sufficiently funded, durable or predictable to matter to the recommended plan.
5. Its economic mechanism is explained and, when possible, quantified.

Do not award points for general political support, nonbinding targets, expired programs, generic infrastructure plans, or incentives whose price cap, origin rule, local-content rule or applicant criteria exclude the case. Mark uncertain eligibility `Unknown / Unverified` and award `+0.0` until confirmed.

Do not double count the same policy. If a policy receives bonus points, do not also increase a base-dimension score solely because that policy exists. Base scores may still use observed market outcomes, but the narrative must distinguish historical demand evidence from the incremental forward policy tailwind.

## Calculation and display

1. Calculate each scorable dimension from verified evidence using the anchors.
2. Calculate the base weighted composite at full precision: `sum(dimension score × weight)`.
3. Add the verified favorable-policy bonus separately.
4. Cap the adjusted result at `10.0`.
5. Round only the final adjusted result to the nearest `0.5`; at an exact quarter-point tie, round upward.
6. Display one decimal place.

Always show:

- base weighted composite;
- favorable-policy bonus and supporting eligibility evidence;
- final adjusted score;
- evidence status for every dimension;
- material Unknown / Unverified items;
- the conditions that could materially move the score.

Use the final score as decision support, not as a substitute for the evidence, unknowns, or validation plan.
